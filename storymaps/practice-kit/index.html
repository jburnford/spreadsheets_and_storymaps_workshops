StoryMaps practice kit

Use these open-access materials to try out the main StoryMaps blocks before you build your own project. Everything here is public domain or openly licensed, so you can use it freely in a practice story as long as you include the credit shown under each item. Click an image to download it.

Practice tip: make a separate practice story (for example, “Practice: Britain's industrial museums”) and try one block from each section below. Don't build this into your project story.

Jump to: Basics · Image · Map · Chart · Infographic · Image gallery · Video · Audio · Embed · Swipe · Timeline · Sidecar · Map tour · Crediting sources

Basics: Text, Button, Separator

  • Text: write a short introduction to your practice story. Try a heading, a paragraph, a bulleted list, and a link.
  • Button: add a button that links to one of the museum websites in the table below.
  • Separator: add one between two sections of your story.

Image

Add one of these images. Then open the image's settings and fill in three things: a caption, the credit (copy it from below), and alt text that describes the image for someone who can't see it. Your final project needs all three for every image.

Audio

Add an audio clip. Two options:

  • A sound effect: “Coal falling into a hopper” (1962, 55 seconds) from the BBC Sound Effects archive. Download it from the BBC site yourself. The BBC allows its sound effects to be used for personal, educational, or research purposes: read the terms on the site, and credit it as “BBC Sound Effects”. It would suit a Big Pit point.
  • A reading: the opening of chapter 2, “A Manchester Tea-Party”, of Elizabeth Gaskell's novel Mary Barton: A Tale of Manchester Life (1848). Read by LibriVox volunteers, public domain.

Embed

An Embed block shows a live web page inside your story. Try embedding the Internet Archive's scan of Baines's 1835 History of the Cotton Manufacture in Great Britain, so readers can page through a primary source:

https://archive.org/details/cottonmanufact00bain

Not every website allows embedding. If one doesn't work, StoryMaps shows a link card instead.

Swipe

Sidecar

A Sidecar keeps one image or map fixed on one side while text scrolls past on the other. Try a sidecar with three slides: use the 1835 engravings from the Image gallery section (for example spinning, then weaving, then the mill from outside) and write two or three sentences for each.

Map tour

A Map tour takes readers from place to place, with an image and text at each stop. Build a tour of the six museums, using the coordinates and descriptions from the Map section and these photos.

Crediting sources

Every image, video, or sound you use needs a credit in your story, usually in the caption. A good credit says who made it, where you found it, and its licence, for example:

Photo: Francis Franklin, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

“Public domain” means anyone can use the item, but you should still say where it came from.
